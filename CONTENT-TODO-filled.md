### "How much does a POS system cost for a small shop?"
- Summary → What a point of sale system really costs a small shop: the monthly software fee, the card processing fee, the hardware, and when a custom system starts to make more sense than a subscription.
- Opening paragraph → For most small shops, a POS system (point of sale: the software and devices you use to take payments and track sales) costs somewhere between nothing and a few hundred dollars a month in software, plus a fee of roughly 2.3% to 3% on every card payment, plus a one time hardware cost that can run from about $60 for a simple card reader to over $1,000 for a full counter setup. The monthly fee is the number most owners compare, but the card fee is usually where most of the money goes.
- Sections →
  - **The three costs you will always pay.** Every POS has the same three parts. The first is the software subscription, which is often free at the basic level and rises with features and the number of locations. The second is the processing fee taken from each card payment. The third is hardware: card readers, a tablet or terminal, a receipt printer, a cash drawer and a barcode scanner. Add the three together before you compare providers.
  - **What popular systems charged in 2026.** As of mid 2026, published comparisons listed Square with a free plan and paid plans from about $49 a month per location, with in person card fees around 2.4% to 2.6% plus 15 cents. Shopify POS Pro was about $89 a month per location, and Lightspeed's retail plans started around $89 a month on annual billing. Clover and Toast sat in a similar range, depending on the package. Prices change often, so always check the provider's own pricing page before deciding.
  - **The card fee is the real cost.** Here is a simple example. A shop that takes $20,000 a month in card payments pays about $500 a month in processing at 2.5%. That is ten times a $49 software plan. When you compare systems, compare the processing rate first.
  - **Hidden costs to ask about.** Ask about fees for extra registers or locations, paid add ons for inventory or loyalty, contract length and cancellation fees, and what happens to your sales data if you leave. Some providers make it hard to export your history.
  - **When a custom POS makes sense.** An off the shelf POS is the right choice for most single shops. A custom system starts to pay off when you run several outlets, sell through distributors, need the POS to talk directly to your warehouse or accounting, or pay a growing monthly bill for features you barely use. In those cases, one system built around how you work can cost less over a few years than stacking subscriptions.
  - **How to decide.** Write down your monthly card sales, the number of registers and locations, and the two or three things you need the system to do. Then ask each provider for the total yearly cost, including processing. If you want a second opinion on whether a custom system would save you money, talk to us.

### "When to move your orders off WhatsApp"
- Summary → WhatsApp is a great way to start taking orders, but it stops working at a certain size. Here are the signs that you have outgrown it, and what a proper ordering system changes.
- Opening paragraph → You should move your orders off WhatsApp when orders start getting lost, when only one person knows what has been ordered, or when you spend more time copying messages into notebooks and spreadsheets than serving customers. WhatsApp is excellent for talking to customers. It was never built to be an order system.
- Sections →
  - **Why WhatsApp works so well at the start.** Your customers already use it, it costs nothing, and it feels personal. For a small shop, a home kitchen or a new restaurant, taking orders by chat is a perfectly sensible way to begin.
  - **Sign 1: Orders get missed.** When a busy evening brings dozens of chats at once, messages scroll out of sight. A missed order is a lost sale and an unhappy customer, and it usually happens on your best days.
  - **Sign 2: Everything lives in one person's phone.** If the person holding the phone is sick, on leave or simply busy, nobody else knows what has been ordered, paid for or delivered. Your business depends on one device.
  - **Sign 3: You copy every order twice.** If orders are typed from chats into a notebook, a spreadsheet or the kitchen, every copy is a chance for a mistake and a few minutes lost.
  - **Sign 4: You can't answer simple questions.** How many orders did you take last month? Which items sell best on Fridays? Who are your regular customers? Scrolling through chats can't tell you.
  - **What a proper ordering system changes.** Customers order from a menu or catalog with clear prices and options. Each order lands in one list your whole team can see, goes straight to the kitchen or packing screen, and records payment automatically. You still keep WhatsApp for conversations, and you can even send order updates there automatically.
  - **A sensible next step.** You don't need to rebuild everything at once. Many businesses start with a simple order page and a shared order screen, then add delivery tracking, loyalty and reports later. If you are not sure where to start, tell us how you take orders today and we will suggest the smallest change that helps most.

### "A checklist before you buy clinic software"
- Summary → Twelve questions to ask before you choose software for appointments, patient records and prescriptions, so you buy a system your doctors and front desk will actually use.
- Opening paragraph → Before you buy clinic software, check four things: that it fits how your clinic actually runs, that patient data is safe and stays yours, that your staff can learn it quickly, and that you know the full cost over several years. Use the checklist below with every provider you talk to.
- Sections →
  - **Fit with how you work.** 1. Does it handle your appointment types, doctors' schedules and walk ins the way you run them today? 2. Can patients book, reschedule and cancel online, and get reminders by SMS or WhatsApp? 3. Does it support your prescriptions, test orders and referrals, in the format your doctors already use?
  - **Patient data and privacy.** 4. Where is patient data stored, and who can access it? Ask for role based access, so a receptionist does not see what only a doctor should. 5. Does it meet the privacy rules where you operate? In the US that means HIPAA (Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act). In Europe it means GDPR (General Data Protection Regulation). 6. Can you export all your data at any time, in a usable format, if you ever leave?
  - **Day to day use.** 7. Can your front desk learn it in a day? Ask for a trial with your real staff. 8. Does it work on the devices you already have, and does it keep working if the internet drops for a few minutes? 9. Is there a mobile view for doctors who move between rooms or branches?
  - **Cost and support.** 10. What is the total cost over three years, including setup, training, extra users and new branches? 11. Who do you call when something breaks, how fast do they respond, and in which time zone? 12. Can it connect to your lab, pharmacy, billing or accounting tools, or will your staff copy data between systems?
  - **Off the shelf or built for you.** Ready made software suits many single doctor practices. A system built around your clinic makes more sense when you run several branches, have your own lab or pharmacy, or keep fighting a tool that was designed for someone else's workflow. If you want help comparing options, we are happy to look at your current setup with you.
